    How to read this map

    The colours are quantiles. Boroughs are sorted and cut into equally sized groups, so each shade holds roughly the same number of boroughs. Equal steps in colour are therefore not equal steps in value; the strip under the menu shows where every borough actually sits.

    Grey is data, not a gap in the map. Six boroughs failed to submit at least one quarterly return and Ealing submitted one in four. A council that cannot say how many households it has in temporary accommodation is telling you something.

    Measured or inferred is marked on every measure. Household counts, stays, children and out-of-area placements are MHCLG statutory returns. Nightly rates are decoded from each council's own published payments and stay inference until an officer checks one against an invoice.

    A blank rate is deliberate. Where a borough batches its invoices, one payment covers many units and cannot be turned into a nightly rate, so none is shown.

    Why this is a shared problem

    Method and confidence

    Household counts, length of stay, children and out-of-area placements are MHCLG statutory returns — measured, not modelled. Nightly rates are decoded from councils' own published payment files and remain inference until an officer confirms one against an invoice.

    Three things this map deliberately will not do

    It will not rank boroughs on total spend. Each borough's published file captures a different and unknowable share of its bill — cost per household-night ranges from £3 to £107, which measures publication practice, not procurement.

    It will not compare an operator's price between boroughs unless both payments sit under the same homelessness label. Ungated, the same firm's payments mix homelessness lets with children's semi-independent placements — different products at different prices.

    It will not show a rate where a borough batches its invoices. A payment covering many units cannot be divided into a nightly rate.
